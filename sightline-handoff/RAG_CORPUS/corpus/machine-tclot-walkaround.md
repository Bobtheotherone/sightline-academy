T-CLOC is the standard pre-ride inspection skeleton: Tires and wheels, Controls and cables, Lights and electrics, Oil and fuel, Chassis. Five zones, same order every time. The power of the acronym is not the letters — it's the ritual. A fixed order walked the same way each ride turns you into a change detector: today's new drip, today's softer lever, today's low tire stands out against yesterday's baseline.

Tires and wheels: pressure by gauge (never by thumb — the pressures are so low that a big loss still looks round), sidewall cuts, missing lug nuts, bent rims. Controls and cables: throttle snaps fully closed on release, both brakes firm, cables free of fraying and kinks, steering sweeps lock to lock without catching. Lights and electrics: headlight, taillight, brake light if equipped, and the engine stop switch actually stops the engine. Oil and fuel: levels checked, and a look underneath for fresh drips — the ground under a parked machine is an honest witness. Chassis: nuts and bolts snug at the fixtures that carry load, suspension moves freely, nothing cracked, nothing hanging.

Most findings are two-minute fixes at home and trailside emergencies later — that timing difference is the entire value of the ritual. A few findings are immediate no-gos regardless of who is waiting: a throttle that won't return, brakes that won't firm up, steering that binds. The walkaround exists precisely to move those discoveries from the trail to the driveway.
