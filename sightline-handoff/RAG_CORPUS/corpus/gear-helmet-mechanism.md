A helmet's save happens inside, not outside. The hard shell spreads a point impact across a wider area, but the work is done by the crushable liner, which collapses over milliseconds to stretch the head's deceleration out in time. Slower deceleration means lower peak force on the brain. One mechanism — and four rules fall straight out of it.

It must fit, because a loose helmet rotates and shifts on the way to the impact and arrives in the wrong position. The fit check: snug all around with no pressure points after several minutes of wear, the forehead skin moves with the shell when you rotate it, and the helmet stays planted during a firm head shake with the strap fastened snug to the throat.

It must be fastened, every ride, because an unbuckled helmet leaves the head before the ground arrives.

It must carry a real safety rating, because the liner's crush behavior is exactly what the rating tests. A novelty shell has the silhouette of protection and none of the mechanism.

It must be retired after a real impact, even if it looks fine. The liner is single-use: crushed foam does not spring back, and a helmet that has done its job once has already spent the material it would need to do it again. Sizing note for parents: children wear correctly-sized youth helmets, never a small adult helmet resting loose.
