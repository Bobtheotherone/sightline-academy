T-CLOC stands for Tires and wheels, Controls and cables, Lights and electrics, Oil and fuel, Chassis — and each of the five zones earns its place by catching a different way a ride goes wrong. Tires and wheels matter because ATV grip lives in low tire pressure, and a pressure loss you cannot see by eye changes handling everywhere. Controls and cables matter because throttle and brakes are the only conversation you have with the machine at speed. Lights and electrics matter at dawn, dusk, and dust, and because the engine stop switch is a safety device that has to work the one time you need it. Oil and fuel matter because a machine that dies far from the trailhead converts a ride into a recovery. Chassis matters because the frame carries every load the other zones generate.

Most walkaround findings are minor: a soft tire aired up, a loose fastener snugged, a low fluid topped. A short list of findings ends the ride before it starts, no matter who is waiting: a throttle that does not snap fully closed on its own, a brake lever that will not firm up, steering that binds or catches anywhere in its sweep, a fuel or oil leak that is actively dripping, or a wheel with missing or loose lug nuts. These are no-gos because each one removes a control you will need at a moment you do not choose. Deeper diagnosis belongs to a qualified mechanic; the walkaround's job is only to notice. Five zones, checked in the same order every ride, is how noticing becomes reliable.
