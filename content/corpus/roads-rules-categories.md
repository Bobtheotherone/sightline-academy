ATV law is local everywhere and uniform nowhere, so the useful knowledge is not a rulebook — it is knowing the categories of rules that exist so you know what questions to ask before riding somewhere new. The recurring categories: minimum operator ages, often with different thresholds by machine size and sometimes with supervision or certification requirements for young riders; helmet and gear mandates, which frequently differ for minors and adults; registration, titling, and OHV permits or trail passes; where riding is allowed — public road prohibitions, designated trail systems, public land classifications, and closures that shift seasonally; equipment requirements such as spark arrestors, working lights, or flags in certain terrain; sound limits; passenger rules tied to machine design; and insurance requirements in some places.

The pattern worth noticing across all of them: nearly every category encodes something the course covers as physics or judgment. Age and size rules encode the fit problem; road prohibitions encode the pavement handling problem; passenger rules encode the single-rider stability budget; light and flag requirements encode the visibility problem.

For any specific question — is this legal, here, for this rider, on this machine — the honest answer has a fixed shape: here is the category of rule in play, and the authoritative source is your local land manager, state or provincial OHV agency, or published local regulations, checked recently, because these rules change and secondhand summaries go stale.
