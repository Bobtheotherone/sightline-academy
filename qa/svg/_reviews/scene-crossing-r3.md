## Reviewer notes
Round 3 review, adversarial. I ran _check.py (exit 0) and _render.py myself and viewed all three delivered PNGs (.img, .rm, .frames), then — because the harness's FRAME_T tops out at 12 s while this file loops at 16 s — I built my own seek harness in the scratchpad and rendered 30 additional paused frames covering 3.7–16 s, plus pixel-accurate 700 px true-size crops of the quad, the stop bar, the sight lines, all eleven rocks, and the tree/road boundary, and a scaled side-by-side against web/src/assets/raster/scene-crossing-768w.png. I also sampled raw pixel values along the sight-line row and the stop-bar row rather than trusting a zoomed crop — that check made me retract an initial "the dots are white" reading and restate it correctly as a paper ring dominating a navy core, which is the defect actually visible at true size.

Where I judged the candidate against the brief rather than the shipped raster: §F explicitly asks for the sight lines to leave "quad 1's helmet", so the origin itself is brief-compliant — what I have written up as P2 is the execution, i.e. dots printed on the #B5446E accent and scattered across the machine, which the shipped plate avoids and which no clause permits. Likewise the trail's low interior contrast (#ECF3EF at ~5 % above the #F9FCFA ground) is inherent to the brief's own colour pins, so I did not book it as a defect; if the owner wants the trail to read the way the shipped plate's tan corridor does, the ground either side needs to carry #ECF3EF and the trail the paper value, which is a brief change, not a build fix.

Two things I could not fully resolve. First, the car's handedness: it is in the far lane heading right, which is wrong-way for right-hand traffic, but the palette forces a pale centre line so the road could equally be read as two same-direction lanes, and the shipped raster has the same arrangement — hence P3 rather than higher, and the fix I propose (near lane) is worth taking anyway because it also repairs the P2 about the lit sight line never meeting the car. Second, the rock verdict: I checked all eleven at true display size rather than sampling, and every one is occluded by a conifer. Under a strict reading of "an element that reads as the wrong thing" that is a P1; I held it at P2 because they are inert background furniture that misleads nobody about the procedure. It is nonetheless the first thing I would fix, and the cheapest.

Verdict is fix on six P2s. None of them touch the file's structure, which is sound — the motion architecture (one 16 s duration per element with percentage beats, staggered machines for a seamless seam, a mask-driven dashoffset reveal) is the best-built of the animated candidates I have seen in this folder, and the reduced-motion contract is exactly right. The work remaining is drawing and placement, not plumbing.
